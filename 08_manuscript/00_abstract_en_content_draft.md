# ABSTRACT

This study examines the cognitive requirements represented in the second-round papers from the 35th to the 42nd editions of the National Physics Competition for High School Students in China and develops corresponding suggestions for instructional translation. Analytically distinct subquestions were used as units of analysis, yielding 219 analysis units and 876 coding records. Following independent coding by two raters, adjudication of disagreements, and record-level evidence review, descriptive statistics were produced for dimensional presence, level composition, and co-occurrence, and representative cases were examined through item and reference-solution evidence. Model construction, scientific reasoning, scientific argumentation, and questioning and innovation occurred in 203, 219, 106, and 84 analysis units, respectively. L1 was the most frequent level among units where model construction, scientific reasoning, or scientific argumentation occurred, whereas L2 was the most frequent level for questioning and innovation. Model construction and scientific reasoning co-occurred in 203 analysis units; scientific argumentation and questioning and innovation co-occurred in 40 units, with a union of 150 units. Six representative cases illustrated condition-relation chains, state classification and comparison, approximation-based modeling, same-object comparison, multi-object constraints, and threshold judgment. These structures were translated into suggestions for learning tasks, scaffolds, and assessment evidence. The study provides an evidence-linked analytical path from subquestion-level corpus records to coding, descriptive statistics, and case analysis, offering a reference for physics competition item research and instructional design. The findings describe cognitive requirements represented by items and reference solutions and do not directly indicate students' actual capabilities or instructional effectiveness.

Keywords: physics competition; scientific thinking; test-item analysis; content analysis; task cognitive requirements
