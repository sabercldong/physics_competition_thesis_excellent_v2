# English Abstract Content Draft (Not a Final School-format Abstract)

**Objective:** This study examines the cognitive requirements represented by tasks in the second-round papers from the 35th to the 42nd editions of the National Physics Competition for High School Students in China. It analyzes four dimensions: model construction, scientific reasoning, scientific argumentation, and questioning and innovation, and prepares traceable case materials for instructional translation. The stage descriptor in this draft is a working translation only and is not asserted as the official English event name.

**Methods:** Analytically distinct subquestions were used as units of analysis, yielding 219 analysis units. The project retains RATER-A/B coding returns, ADJ-01 adjudications, ADJ-02 corrections, and item-level question-solution evidence links. The current versioned coding table contains 876 records and is used to produce descriptive statistics for dimensional presence, level composition, and co-occurrence.

**Results:** Under the current coding rules, model construction, scientific reasoning, scientific argumentation, and questioning and innovation occurred in 203, 219, 106, and 84 analysis units, respectively. L1 was the most frequent level among the units where model construction, scientific reasoning, or scientific argumentation occurred, whereas L2 was the most frequent level for questioning and innovation. Model construction and scientific reasoning co-occurred in 203 analysis units. Scientific argumentation and questioning and innovation co-occurred in 40 analysis units, with a union of 150 units. The evidence links also support three task-structure cases: condition-relation chains, state classification-energy relations, and modeling with approximation conditions. These cases inform task, scaffold, and assessment-design suggestions.

**Conclusion:** The material package provides a traceable path from subquestion-level corpus records and coding evidence to descriptive statistics and case materials. The results describe cognitive requirements represented by the items and their reference solutions; they do not indicate students' actual capabilities, instructional effectiveness, causal effects, long-term trends, or score differences for the 35th edition.

**Limitations:** The identities, independence, and actual work processes of RATER-A/B, ADJ-01, ADJ-02, and R01 are recorded only through self-declarations and cannot be independently verified by this project. Full-text verification remains incomplete for key SOLO sources, competition studies, and some methodological literature. No teacher, student, or classroom data were collected. This draft must be checked against the current ECNU professional-degree format, required length, keyword rules, official event terminology, and AI-disclosure requirements before final submission.
